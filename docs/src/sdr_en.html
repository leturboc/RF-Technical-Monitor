An SDR (software-defined radio: HackRF, SDRplay RSP1B, RTL-SDR, Airspy, WiNRADiO…) receives a window of a few MHz at once, without gaps, and can demodulate: you hear what is on a frequency. The tinySA sweeps a whole band but listens to nothing. The two complement each other.

Three modes

The mode button, in the top bar, chooses what you use:

Each mode has a base layout and preset views (listen, monitor, analyse, dual screen). Then move the panels as you like: your layout is remembered, and it follows your user profile.

The four panels

  • Receiver: ▶ / ■, sound, the big A frequency (scroll wheel on a digit to change it), the meter in dBm with a word (very weak, weak, good, strong, too strong) — click the meter = needle or bar, your choice —, the sound meter (graduated in dB, −18 / −20 dBFS alignment mark, PRE / POST volume), and two small windows to choose from: channel (spectrum, I/Q constellation — FM draws a circle —, phase, instantaneous frequency = the modulation) and sound (spectrum, L/R oscilloscope, goniometer with the L/R correlation), ⭐ favourites, patrol, alarms, the “what is it” info and the pilot.
  • Spectrum: the SDR window. Your mics are marked by name. Click = listen (A), right-click = B. Previous / next mic, zoom, peak hold.
  • Waterfall: the history of the window, A and B as lines.
  • My mics: this SDR's mics with their level; double-click = listen, right-click = menu. By default it follows all your mics (those of its scenes); to follow only some: select them in My frequencies then Ctrl+Shift+F, or right-click › 📡 Follow with the SDR, or the panel's ＋ Add my selection button. Remove (Del) takes them out, ↺ All goes back to all. Chosen mics outside the SDR window stay listed (greyed, click = go there); the patrol only goes through them.

Simple, Plus, Expert

The app's display level also applies to the SDR station. Simple: listen, favourites, patrol, alarms. Plus: demodulation, step, squelch, receiver B, recordings. Expert: FFT size, device settings, load and saturation.

The first time, open 🔌 First SDR connection (mode menu, or 🔌 Hardware › 📡 SDR). The wizard does six steps and says ✔, ⚠ or ✖ for each, with what to do:

  1. My SDR: choose the exact model. It sets the rates, gains and frequency range.
  2. Find it: the SDR tool is looked for, the device detected, a signal received for 2 s and the rate checked. If a line is ✖, it says why (missing tool, USB driver, SDR taken by another program, USB port too slow).
  3. Set the gain automatically: each input gain value is tried; the app keeps the highest that doesn't saturate, with 6 dB of headroom. If it saturates even at the lowest, it advises an attenuator.
  4. Listen to a mic: the station starts on one of your mics: you should hear it.
  5. Calibrate the levels (optional): see the chapter “Calibrating the levels on the tinySA”.
  6. Diagnostics: a file sums everything up (model, gains, results, tool messages). Send it to support if something gets stuck.
The SDR tools (hackrf_transfer, rtl_sdr, SoapySDR…) come from the SDR kit to unzip next to the app, or from PothosSDR. The SDRplay also needs the maker's API, the WiNRADiO its software.

An analog wireless mic is listened to like a radio: the station demodulates the FM and plays the sound. Two quick ways to tune to a mic:

  • Double-click its name or frequency in “My frequencies” (in SDR or combined mode);
  • click it in the SDR spectrum, or in “My mics”.

The listening setting is automatic

Each device transmits its own way: wide FM, de-emphasis, compander (compressor at the transmitter, expander at the receiver). The app guesses the right setting from what it knows about the mic: its device in the imported plan (EazyRF, WWB, WSM…), its group name, its name. The 🎧 Listening line shows “auto (from the mic)”. If you change the preset or the compander, your choice is remembered for this mic (“✋ chosen for this mic”), in the list and in shows.

Digital systems (ULX-D, Axient Digital, QLX-D, SLX-D, EW-D / EW-DX, 6000, Spectera…) can't be listened to: their sound is coded and often encrypted. The app says so (📵) and keeps monitoring their level. Manufacturers don't publish their companders: the app's are approximations, tuned to sound pleasant, not exact copies.

Receivers A and B

At Plus level, a second receiver B listens to another frequency of the same window. “A left / B right” puts each in one ear of the headphones.

Squelch, volume, recording

The squelch cuts the hiss when nothing is on. ⏺ Sound records what you hear as WAV, ⏺ IQ records the raw signal of the whole window (large files, to replay in Real time). Both ask for your consent the first time (voices can be recorded).

Favourites on the numeric keypad

Put your most important mics on keys 1 to 9 of the numeric keypad: right-click a ⭐ button of the Receiver (“put the listened mic here”) or right-click a mic in “My mics”. Then one key = the SDR tunes to that mic, with its listening setting, wherever you are in the app.

🔁 Patrol

The patrol moves the SDR from one favourite to the next (or through all this SDR's mics), a few seconds each, in frequency order (the SDR re-centres as little as possible). Switched-off 💤 or paused mics are skipped. A keypad key, a click on a frequency or a double-click stops the patrol: you took over.

The SDR window also monitors your frequencies: the mics in it get their level, states and alarms, as with the tinySA. If a tinySA sweeps at the same time, it keeps the monitoring (the two scales aren't mixed).

🚨 Tune to alarms

When a new alarm comes in (conflict, intermod, absent, too strong, pilot) on a mic of this SDR, the station tunes to it by itself: you hear what's going on, and the log notes what the SDR sees there (“what is it”). It doesn't steal a frequency you just chose (30 s), and it stays at least 20 s on an alarm before moving to the next. ↩ Go back takes you back to where you were. If the SDR monitors alone and the alarm is outside its window, it doesn't move by itself (the other mics would lose their measurement): it offers 🚨 Go to the alarm. Tick “and record the sound” to keep a WAV of the alarm (10 s before if the SDR was already on that mic, 20 s after).

🏷 What is it?

Under the frequency, the app says what it sees on A from the shape of the spectrum: analog FM mic, narrow FM (walkie-talkie), pure carrier, digital block, TV… with the occupied width and the dB above the noise. It's a useful guess for sorting, not a certain identification.

🔊 The pilot: “it really is your transmitter”

Many analog mics transmit an inaudible tone, the pilot. The first time the SDR hears it on a mic, it asks “is it really your transmitter?”: ✔ Yes remembers it, ✖ No ignores it (an interferer can have a pilot too). Then: “✔ it really is your transmitter”. If a carrier is there without its pilot for 5 s, a PILOT alarm is raised: maybe another transmitter on your frequency. A pack without a pilot simply shows “no pilot seen”. Right-click the info line = forget the pilot (if you changed pack).

The 📡 SDR · Diagnostics panel (one per station, tabbed next to “My frequencies”) tells what is REALLY going on with your SDR. At the top: where the data comes from (📡 observed = real SDR, 🧪 simulated = SDR demo) and the stream quality. Four tabs:

  • Events: the station's timestamped history, with A's frequency and level at that moment: start, stop, SDR lost then back, reconnections, lost samples, stream dropouts, input saturation, invalid samples, inconsistent timestamps, carrier without audio (a transmitter transmitting without modulating: pack on mute, mic unplugged from the pack). Problems also go to the log (“SDR STREAM”). “Problems only” filters, 💾 CSV… and 📄 Report… (HTML or PDF) export.
  • Device: model, serial number, state (running, reconnecting…), capabilities, set rate and rate actually received, last communication, reconnections, last tool errors.
  • System: CPU and memory of the app, station load (and the share of real computing), processed rate, time of each step.
  • Session: at each ▶, two CSV files (a line of readings every 2 s + the events) in logs/sessions_sdr/<date>/. No audio: a few KB per hour. Can be turned off.

An event is not necessarily a fault: cross-check with what you were hearing. On the SDR spectrum, 📌 Reference now compares with the normal variation: “within normal variation (± x dB)” or “⚠ notable difference”, so a simple fluctuation is not taken for an anomaly. Receiver small windows: new view “I and Q over time” (blue = I, purple = Q).

📡 My SDRs adds stations (up to 4): each has its name, model, serial number (to tell them apart) and its scenes. An SDR attached to a scene only handles that scene's mics, and it answers the keypad and the alarms of those mics. Each station has its four panels, titled with its name.

On the 🗺 site map, SDRs are tiles like the tinySAs: drag an SDR onto a scene so it monitors it; the line between them shows who monitors what.

An SDR measures in dBFS (relative to its converter), not in dBm. Without calibration, its levels are approximate. 📏 Calibrate the SDR levels on the tinySA makes them comparable:

  1. Run the tinySA and the SDR at the same time (combined mode), antennas in the same place.
  2. Switch on mics in the SDR window (at least 2 signals seen by both are needed).
  3. ▶ Measure: for 15 s, the app compares the power of each signal (over ± 100 kHz) seen by both.
  4. It shows the correction (and the spread between signals). ✔ Apply keeps it for this SDR model.

Then the SDR's thresholds, alarms and levels are in real dBm, consistent with the tinySA. Do it again if you change antenna, cable or gain.

The SDR station keeps the last minutes (1, 3 or 5, or off) in memory: the spectrum and the sound heard. ⏪ Go back in time (Receiver button) shows the waterfall of those minutes, most recent at the top, with alarms as markers.

  • Click the waterfall = pick a moment; the window says which mic A was on at that time.
  • ▶ Replay from here: up to 60 s of what you were hearing.
  • 💾 Keep this passage: the sound (WAV) and the image, in the recordings folder.
Everything stays in memory and disappears when you close the app, except what you keep. Sound takes about 11 MB per minute and per station.

No SDR yet? 🧪 SDR demo (mode menu) runs the whole station without hardware: the app makes the signals of your mics, each with a sound (voice “one, two”, melody, beeps…) and a pilot. To make it lively: an interferer passes on your first “must stay free” frequency, a mic switches off now and then, another loses its pilot, and two TV channels are there. You can try everything: listening, favourites, patrol, alarms, going back in time, several SDRs.

Right-click a mic in “My mics” › 🎵 Sound of this mic in the SDR demo to give it the sound of your choice (see “Sounds, generator and alarms”).

The tinySA demo mode makes the same signals (all your mics, the jammer, the dropouts, the DTV): you see what your list looks like on the spectrum. In the 🎵 Audio generator, “Use this sound for…” › a mic of your list: its sound AND what it transmits in both demos (FM mic, digital, dropouts, jammer, nothing).

🧪 SDR lab (mode menu, or SDR submenu) is a test bench: the same signals and the same measurements for all models, and each result says where it comes from: 🧪 simulated, 📡 observed (real SDR) or 🧮 computed (formula). Five panels, like the SDR ones: tabbed at the bottom the first time, then wherever you put them (drag them, pull them out as real windows, send them to another screen via 🪟 Windows or 🖥 Several screens). ⛶ Lab view (mode menu, or button in the Chain panel): the window shows only the lab, either ▤ tidy (one panel at a time, in tabs: the default), ▦ everything on screen (grid) or 🖥🖥 on 2 screens (chain and live on your screen, companders, modulations and faults on the other; one screen = grid); “↩ Leave the Lab view” to go back. Each graph can be pulled out or removed: right-click › “⧉ Pull this graph out” (its own window, “🖥 Next screen”, “📌 Put back”), “✕ Hide this graph” (remembered next launch), “↺ Restore all graphs”. In 🖥 Several screens, the “🧪 Lab on the other screen” preset puts the whole lab, in a grid, on the 2nd screen:

  • ① Full chain: a wireless mic step by step. Source audio → transmitter compressor → FM modulation (IQ, noise, fault) → demodulation → expander → output audio. Six synchronised views (audio in / out, level over time, I and Q, constellation, IQ spectrum, instantaneous frequency) and the measurements: levels, difference, residual error, delay, pumping. Pick a fault (lost blocks, saturation, interferer next door, pilot disappearing, weak signal, drifting transmitter) to see what it does and what the app detects. Try a different compander at transmit and receive: the dynamics are wrong, and the lab says so.
  • ② Compare companders: tick several models. For each, the same tests: measured transfer curve (a sine staircase, with the formula dotted), 20 dB level jump (attack and release times, overshoot), frequency response (EQ) and phase of the chain (pure delay removed), and your source passed through (level, error, pumping). Each model has its sheet: maker, technology, documentation, implementation, fidelity, limits, version. 📄 Comparison report writes all of it as HTML: conditions, sources, models and versions, indicators, differences, limits of validity.
  • ③ Live (SDR): while the SDR station listens to an FM mic on A, the received audio before the expander goes through each ticked model in parallel: level, EQ and phase of each, live. 🎧 Listen with the chosen model switches A's listening to that compander (comparison by ear, A/B). EQ and phase are only drawn where the received audio allows a measurement (a voice doesn't contain every frequency).
  • ④ Modulations: the same audio as wide FM, narrow FM, pure carrier, AM, digital block and DTT: what each looks like on a spectrum, and what the “What is this signal?” detection guesses (it has no “AM” class).
  • ⑤ Simulated faults: inject a fault into the live 🧪 SDR demo (lost blocks, frozen stream, saturation, SDR unplugged, interferer on a mic, pilots cut, strong noise) for 5 s to 5 min, to watch the Receiver's Stream line, the alarms and the log react. Everything is marked SIMULATED; nothing touches a real SDR.

Guided scenarios and faults

Simulated faults panel › 🎬 Guided scenarios: one click sets the full chain, computes it, places the fault in the SDR demo if it is running, and says what to look at and why: all is well (the reference), compander forgotten at the receiver, wrong compander, mic far away, interferer next door, USB overloaded, input too strong, pack on mute (carrier without audio). Extra live faults: audio source unavailable and inconsistent timestamps; each is noted in the station history (Diagnostics panel).

Measuring your real transmitter

This is the only way to get a real reference. In the 🎵 Audio generator, choose “Test signal: level staircase” (a marker silence then 9 steps of 1 kHz, from −40 to 0 dBFS) and feed it by cable into the pack's audio input. Listen to that mic on the SDR station (A), wait about twenty seconds, then tab ③ › 📏 Measure now: the slope of the received steps gives the transmitter's real compression ratio (2:1, 1.5:1…), marked 📡 observed, and included in the report.

Good to know. Brand companders are not published: the “approximated” models (Sennheiser, Shure, Wisycom, Lectrosonics) are reproductions by judgement, not the real processing. The lab compares MODELS. And audio received by an SDR may have gone through other processing (limiter, transmitter filters): without a known reference, a difference cannot be put down to the compander alone.

Stream quality, persistence and reference

The Receiver's Stream line tells whether the SDR delivers everything: lost samples (USB or PC can't keep up), dropouts, input saturation, reconnections. An SDR that drops out is restarted automatically (after 3, 10 then 30 s). On the SDR spectrum: Persistence (recent spectra fade slowly: an intermittent signal glows) and 📌 Reference (freezes the spectrum as a dashed line, then shows the difference at A and the largest change). 📋 All SDRs (SDR settings): what the app can do with each model.

Transmitting over the air without authorisation is forbidden. This feature is for working on a cable, with an attenuator of 30 dB or more before the receiver's or tinySA's input.

A HackRF can transmit (SDRplay, RTL-SDR, Airspy and WiNRADiO only receive). 📡 Transmit with a HackRF sends up to 8 signals at once in a window of a few MHz:

  • FM mic with sound and pilot (test a receiver, its squelch, the app's listening);
  • walkie-talkie (narrow FM), pure carrier, wide block (digital, TV);
  • intermittent: on 1 s every 4 s, like an interferer that comes and goes (hunting practice, alarm tests);
  • several carriers at once: deliberate intermodulation, to watch on the tinySA.

The transmit gain starts at 0 dB and the amp is off: on a cable, that's plenty. Transmission stops by itself (1 to 30 min), when the window or the app closes, and it is logged. The “on a cable” box is required. A HackRF receives OR transmits: stop the SDR station using it before transmitting.

With the SDR demo on, transmission is simulated: nothing goes out, and the SDR demo “receives” what you transmit. Handy to try everything without hardware.

  • An SDR can only be used by one program at a time: close the other program (and the app's SDR windows using it) before starting the station.
  • An SDR's window is a few MHz wide (HackRF up to ~15 MHz, RSP1B ~8 MHz, RTL-SDR ~2 MHz): when A leaves the window, the station re-centres by itself.
  • A HackRF has 8 bits: a very strong transmitter nearby saturates it quickly. The automatic gain setting and an attenuator fix that.
  • An SDR's levels are only in real dBm after calibration on the tinySA (or for a WiNRADiO, factory calibrated).
  • Recorded sound and IQ are kept for the time you choose (📡 SDR settings › recordings kept), then deleted.
