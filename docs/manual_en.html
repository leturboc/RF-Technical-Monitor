TinySA Monitor — user manual

Version 7.11 · for the tinySA Ultra on Windows · RF coordination for festivals, concerts and events.

In one sentence: the app keeps sweeping your bands with a tinySA, compares what it sees with your frequency list, and warns you as soon as a transmitter goes quiet or a signal shows up where it should not.

1. What is it?

TinySA Monitor does two jobs at once:

  • Spectrum analyser: live spectrum over several bands, max hold, waterfall, live zoom, venue reference curve, TV channel guide and allocations per country.
  • Frequency monitor: 100+ frequencies, each with a status (“must transmit” or “must stay free”). The app works out the state of each one, raises alarms (sound, panel, phone), keeps a log and can record the whole show so you can look at it again afterwards.

On top of that, coordination tools: intermodulation, plan check in the style of EazyRF, backup frequency, line check, a web page for the team.

The band allocations shown (DTT, 4G/5G, aero…) are for guidance only: always check the local regulations before transmitting.

2. Install and start

Getting the exe

  1. On the Releases page of the GitHub repository, download TinySA-Monitor.exe from the latest version (or from the “Test” pre-release to try new features).
  2. Double-click it. Windows may show “Windows protected your PC” (unsigned exe): More info › Run anyway.
  3. No installation: the exe runs as it is. Your settings are kept in %APPDATA%\TinySAMonitor\.

First start

  1. Plug the tinySA Ultra in over USB and close tinySA-App if it is open (only one program at a time on the port).
  2. At the top, pick the Port: the one marked ✔ tinySA is detected automatically. No tinySA at hand? Tick Demo mode: simulated signals so you can try everything.
  3. Click ▶ Start. The spectrum fills up and the tinySA version is shown next to the port.
Tip: ☰ More › Expert mode shows every setting (RBW, LNA, attenuator, segments…). In simple mode you only get what matters during the show.

4. Preparing a show

Bands to sweep

  • Settings › Bands to sweep: tick the bands to monitor, or add some (start, stop, step). Presets exist (UHF mics, Spectera, 1G4…).
  • More points means finer detail but a longer cycle. The app estimates how long a cycle takes.
  • Country and frequency plan: pick your country to get the right TV channels and allocations on the spectrum.

Your frequencies

  • 📥 Import: frequency plan from EazyRF, WWB, WSM, Excel… The import recognises the columns and suggests names. You can also drag and drop a CSV onto the window.
  • Or type them in by hand (＋ Add above the list, or right-click on the spectrum › “Add to my list”).
  • Quick naming: 🔧 Tools › ✏ Name the selection (HF 1, HF 2…, numbered automatically), or Ctrl+D to number downwards from the first selected row.

Statuses: the key to everything

StatusMeaningAlarm
In use (must transmit)one of your transmitters should be there ⚠ MISSING if it stays silent longer than “TX missing after”
Free (must stay free)spare, backup frequency, area to protect ⚠ CONFLICT at the slightest signal
No statusplain monitoringnone (shows busy / free)

With the keyboard, in the list: select rows, then Ctrl+E = In use, Ctrl+1…9 = status no. 1 to 9, Ctrl+0 = none. Your own statuses: ☰ More › 🏷 Statuses.

Scenes and groups

  • Sort your frequencies into Scenes › Groups (e.g. Main stage › Vocal mics). Clicking a scene or a group = only that is monitored and scanned; the Global button brings everything back.
  • Right-click a group: alarm priority. Critical ❗ = sound repeated every 10 s until acknowledged; Normal; Info (no sound).

Thresholds

  • Busy: above it, the frequency is busy. Weak: in between, weak signal.
  • TX missing after: how many seconds without a signal before ⚠ MISSING.
  • “Too strong” alert (−25 dBm by default, purple line): ⚠ TOO STRONG, risk of receiver overload (walkie-talkie right next to the antenna…).
  • A threshold for one frequency only: right-click › “Frequency's own threshold”.
  • Unit: dBm, dBµV or % like WSM (Settings › Detection). Files stay in dBm.
  • Expert: threshold over reference = busy when the level exceeds the measured noise floor of the venue by X dB.

Saving the show (🎪)

🎪 Shows › Save (Ctrl+S) keeps frequencies, scenes, statuses, bands, thresholds, country, position names, venue reference and the details (name, venue, dates, notes). Next year: Open a show, then Save as… for the new edition. A show does not touch your preferences (colours, windows, alerts, language…).

5. Monitoring during the show

  1. ▶ Start. Every sweep updates the list, the wall, the markers and the alarms.
  2. Watch the Signal column (or the wall): everything should be green.
  3. An alarm? The 🔔 turns red and the Alarms panel opens. Acknowledge silences the sound but keeps the alarm (orange) as long as the problem is there.
  4. A strange signal on the spectrum? Right-click it: ⚡ Where could it come from? (intermod), 🔍 view in zoom, ＋ add to my list.
  • Live zoom: double-click a frequency's level or signal, or right-click › 🔍 Live zoom. A tight, fast sweep around it; “Live zoom” again to go back.
  • 📍 Markers (above the spectrum): which markers to show (by state, scene, status, alarms always visible, full line or small discreet marker). Ctrl+M hides / shows them all.
  • 📏 Reference (above the spectrum) › Capture: the venue's noise floor, to be done before the audience arrives. It is drawn dotted and used by the “over reference” threshold.
  • The log records everything (alarms, back to normal, changes), automatically saved as CSV.

6. Reading states and alarms

StatusSignal presentNo signal
Must transmitOK · “OK (weak)” “silent”, then ⚠ MISSING
Must stay free⚠ CONFLICT (or INTERMOD) ✔ free
No statusBUSY (no room here) free
  • INTERMOD: a signal on a “must stay free” frequency that matches an intermodulation product of your transmitters. The formula is in the alarm (e.g. “2×HF 1 − HF 3”).
  • ⚠ DEVICE: a tinySA dropped out (cable, frozen). The app retries on its own every 5 s.
  • ⚠ TOO STRONG: signal above the alert level, risk of overload.
  • The words and colours can be changed: ☰ More › 🔤 Words and colours.

7. The frequency wall

  • ☰ More › 🧱 Wall (or the tab next to the spectrum): one tile per frequency, colour = signal state.
  • 📋 Choose: which frequencies get a tile. “Alarms always visible” brings back a removed frequency while it is in alarm.
  • Tile size: Auto (fills the space), or Small / Medium / Large (scrolls).
  • 🗗 Detach: the wall in its own window, at the size you want; 📌 Dock to put it back.
  • 🖥 Other screen (with 2 screens); F11 = full screen, Esc to go back.

8. Windows and dual screen

Ready-made layouts

☰ More › 🪟 Windows and layouts: Standard, Analyser only, Monitoring, Dual screen (the list goes to the other screen). Each panel can also be shown / hidden.

Real windows

☰ More › 🪟 Windows and layouts › 🗗 Real windows / dual screen, then for each panel (Spectrum, Waterfall, List, Log, Alarms, Settings):

  • Free window: a real Windows window (maximise, minimise, taskbar) that you place wherever you like with the mouse.
  • Screen N: maximised or full screen: sent straight to the chosen screen.
  • In the window: 🖥 Next screen, ⛶ Full screen (F11, Esc), 📌 Dock. Closing the window = docking the panel back.
  • Windows reopen in the same place at the next start. “📌 Dock all” or the Standard layout puts everything back into the main window.
Why not just “detach” a panel? A panel detached the old way stays a Qt “tool” window: no maximise button, always on top, and it re-docks when you brush past an edge. Real windows don't have those problems.

9. Coordination: intermod, plan, backup

🧮 Check the plan (like EazyRF)

  • 🔧 Tools › 🧮 Check the plan. For each frequency: ✔ compatible or ✗ with the details (spacing too small, 2A − B, A + B − C, 5th order).
  • Default device and Device column: a profile per frequency (Wisycom very selective, Sennheiser, Shure, IEMs…) with its minimum spacing, receiver filter, tolerances and step. Select rows › “Apply to selected rows”.
  • Rules: “Like EazyRF” (2 TX ±100 kHz, 3 TX ±50 kHz) by default, Simple, Careful, Very careful or by hand. The sentence “We check: …” says exactly what is counted.
  • On a ✗ row: 🔍 Suggest a compatible frequency (on the device's step, avoiding what the tinySA sees as busy), then ✔ Replace.

⚡ Intermodulation

Products of your transmitters (2, 3, 5 TX; orders 3, 5, 7): what lands on what, with the measured level and a verdict 🔴 🟠 🟡 ⚪. Or the other way round: “where could this signal come from?”. 👁 Monitor adds products to your list as “Free”.

🆘 Backup frequency

Free channels according to the measured spectrum, with no intermod against your list, one click away when a mic gets interference.

10. Line check

Ctrl+L or 🔧 Tools › 🎤: switch your transmitters on one by one; each frequency turns ✓ as soon as its signal arrives. A summary “14 / 16 OK, missing: …” goes into the log. An unknown signal right next to a missing frequency points to a pack set to the wrong channel.

11. Spectera / WMAS (wideband carriers)

  • 🔧 Tools › 📶 Add a Spectera / WMAS block: pick the TV channel or a centre frequency + width (6 or 8 MHz). Or right-click a frequency › “Wide block”.
  • “Must transmit”: the median level of the block is used; “must stay free”: the peak.
  • Intermod and the plan check take the width of the blocks into account.

12. Two tinySAs, network relay

  1. Settings › 2nd tinySA: tick “Use a 2nd tinySA” and give its port (COM4) or network address.
  2. On another PC: run TinySA-Monitor.exe --relais (or 🔧 Tools › 📡 Network relay), choose the port, “Share”, then type the address shown (e.g. 192.168.1.20:5025) on the main PC.
  3. Bands table, “tinySA” column: Auto, 1 or 2. ✂ Split cuts a large band in two.
  4. Position names (Stage, FOH…): shown in the alarms (“seen by FOH”).
  5. ⚖ Calibrate the 2nd against the 1st: same signal into both (splitter), ~20 s, the offset is set automatically.

If one tinySA drops out, the other takes over all the bands (⚠ DEVICE). On a network, prefer a cable to Wi-Fi.

13. Phone alerts and web page

📣 Push alerts

  • 🔧 Tools › 📣 Alerts: ntfy (free app, no account: subscribe to the channel name shown) or Telegram (bot from @BotFather; “🔎 Find my chat id” does it for you).
  • A step-by-step 📖 guide button, and “Send a test alert”. Internet is needed (4G, venue Wi-Fi).
  • Wi-Fi with a login portal (hotel, festival) often blocks ntfy: switch the phone to 4G.
  • Alerts are sent in the language of the interface.

🌐 Web page for the team

  • Same window: enable the web page; the team opens the address shown (http://192.168.x.x:8080) on their phones.
  • Views to choose from: list, wall, live spectrum, waterfall, alarms. Optional password. The page follows the language of the interface.
  • List of connected people: rename, 👋 identify (that client's screen flashes), disconnect everyone.
  • Acknowledge from the phone with a PIN code.

14. After the show: playback, report

  • ⏪ Playback: look at the spectrum at any moment (last 30 minutes in memory, or the recordings on disk), time slider, fast playback, jump to an event.
  • 📄 Show report: standalone HTML (alarms, occupancy, log), printable to PDF.
  • 📤 Export frequencies by scene / group.

15. Other tools

  • 🔁 Convert a scan: WSM, WWB or CSV of your choice (columns, units, separator), as input and as output; or the current scan.
  • 📏 Import a scan as reference (WSM / WWB).
  • 🩺 tinySA diagnostics: a file with the latest exchanges with the tinySA, to send along with a bug report (also written automatically when a tinySA drops out).
  • 🎨 Colours (themes, red night mode), 🏷 Statuses, My vocabulary (quick names).

17. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Port not found / “access denied”Close tinySA-App (one program at a time), replug the USB, ⟳ button next to the port.
The tinySA stops answering after a whileThe app retries on its own. After 3 failed attempts: unplug / replug the tinySA (it can freeze, also with tinySA-App).
A band does not fill up🔧 Tools › 🩺 Diagnostics, and send the file.
Window lost off-screen (screen unplugged)☰ More › 🪟 Windows and layouts › Standard layout (docks everything).
The app crashedSend %APPDATA%\TinySAMonitor\crash.log. The configuration is saved every minute, with a backup copy.

18. Where are my files?

  • Settings: %APPDATA%\TinySAMonitor\ (config.json + config.json.bak, reference.json, crash.log).
  • Shows: Documents\TinySA Monitor\Shows\ (.tsashow files).
  • Logs, recordings, reports, diagnostics: Documents\TinySA Monitor\Journaux\ (can be changed in the app).

19. Language

☰ More › 🌐 Language / Langue: Français or English. The choice is saved straight away and applied at the next start (the app offers to restart). On the very first start, the app follows the Windows language: French if Windows is in French, English otherwise. Frequency names, scenes and groups are yours and are never translated (the suggested names are in English). Files written automatically during the show (log CSV, recordings, shows) keep the same format whatever the language, so a show saved in French opens in English and the other way round.

20. What is not verified

The app is tested automatically (logic, real interface on Windows, fake tinySA), and has run for 2 hours in the field with a real tinySA Ultra. Not yet proven in real conditions:

  • two real tinySAs at the same time, the relay on a real festival network, a full night;
  • real sending of ntfy / Telegram alerts, the alarm sounds, a real dual-screen setup;
  • device profiles other than Wisycom in the plan check (starting values);
  • the level of a real Spectera carrier on the tinySA;
  • the EazyRF / WWB / WSM list export formats (generic guesses);
  • the English interface has been checked automatically (every text has a translation), not yet by an English-speaking user in the field.
