2. What is it?

TinySA Monitor does two jobs at once:

  • Spectrum analyser: live spectrum over several bands, max hold, waterfall, live zoom, venue reference curve, TV channel guide and allocations per country.
  • Frequency monitor: 100+ frequencies, each with a status (“must transmit” or “must stay free”). The app works out the state of each one, raises alarms (sound, panel, phone), keeps a log and can record the whole session so you can look at it again after the show.

On top of that: coordination tools (plan check in the style of EazyRF, intermodulation, backup frequency, line check), a web page for the team, alerts on phones.

The band allocations shown (DTT, 4G/5G, aero…) are for guidance only: always check the local regulations (Ofcom, ANFR, BNetzA…) before transmitting.

3. The equipment

What you need at minimum

  • A tinySA Ultra (the « Ultra » model, with the big screen, up to 6 GHz). It is a small spectrum analyser: a device that measures radio power frequency by frequency. The app tells it what to measure and fetches the results over USB.
  • Its antenna (supplied), screwed onto the RF socket. For mics (470–700 MHz), the telescopic antenna or a small UHF antenna is enough. An antenna for the wrong band sees badly: the measured level is lower than reality.
  • A USB-C cable that carries data. Careful: many USB-C cables only charge. If the PC does not see the tinySA, change the cable first.
  • A Windows PC (10 or 11). No need for a racing machine; a screen of at least 1280 × 720.

Optional

  • A 2nd tinySA: twice as fast (they share the bands), or one per stage (chapter « Two tinySAs, network relay »).
  • A Raspberry Pi (Pi 4 recommended) + a USB power bank: the « roaming tinySA » kit to scan each stage or hunt a jammer while the PC stays at the FOH. The software kit comes with each version (relais-raspberry-pi.zip).
  • A phone: to receive alarms (ntfy, Telegram) and see the web page (list, wall, spectrum, FOH, hunt, field).
  • A 2nd screen: for the frequency wall at the FOH.
  • A small Wi-Fi router: your own network for the PC, the Pi and the phones, more reliable than a venue's Wi-Fi.
Where to put the tinySA? Like a mic receiver: high up, in the clear, near the receiving antennas but not right next to a transmitter (a transmitter 10 cm away saturates the measurement and shows fake signals). Away from LED screens, power supplies and computers, which raise the noise floor.

4. Install and start

Getting the exe

  1. On the Releases page of the GitHub repository, download TinySA-Monitor.exe from the latest version (or from the “Test” pre-release to try new features).
  2. Double-click it. Windows may show “Windows protected your PC” (unsigned exe): More info › Run anyway.
  3. No installation: the exe runs as it is. Your settings are kept in %APPDATA%\TinySAMonitor\.

First start

  1. Plug the tinySA Ultra in over USB and close tinySA-App if it is open (only one program at a time on the port).
  2. At the top, pick the Port: the one marked ✔ tinySA is detected automatically. No tinySA at hand? Tick Demo mode: simulated signals so you can try everything.
  3. Click ▶ Start. The app offers to record the session (chapter 30); then the spectrum fills up and the tinySA version is shown next to the port.
Three display levels (☰ More › 👁 Display): 🟢 Ultra simple (on the very first start) = the spectrum, your list and the alarms, nothing else; 🔵 Simple = + essential settings, the log and sorting by scene / group; 🎛 Expert = every setting (RBW, LNA, attenuator, segments, measurement window, calibration…).
🎓 Tutorial (button on the workflow bar, or ☰ More › Help): six guided tours for beginners (see the chapter « Tutorials, glossary and tooltips »). Start with « 🖥 Discover the screen ».

Part B — Understanding RF

7. RF basics

You don't need to be an engineer, but these few ideas avoid 90 % of the bad surprises.

dBm: the received power

  • dBm measures a power relative to 1 milliwatt, on a logarithmic scale: 0 dBm = 1 mW, −30 dBm = 1 µW, −90 dBm = 1 picowatt.
  • +3 dB = twice the power, +10 dB = ten times, +20 dB = a hundred times. A signal at −50 dBm is 1,000 times stronger than one at −80 dBm.
  • Orders of magnitude with a decent antenna: wireless mic a few metres away −30 to −50 dBm, at 30 m −60 to −75 dBm; tinySA noise floor −100 to −110 dBm; DTT from a distant transmitter −60 to −80 dBm.
  • Other units in the app (⚙ Settings › Thresholds and alarms › Unit): dBµV = dBm + 107 (50 Ω), % like Sennheiser WSM (0 % = −120 dBm, 100 % = 0 dBm). Files always stay in dBm.

The noise floor (the “carpet”)

Even with no transmitter, the tinySA sees a minimum level: its own noise plus the venue's (LED walls, power supplies, Wi-Fi…). A signal is only usable if it clearly stands out of that carpet: that's the signal-to-noise ratio. A mic receiver typically needs 20 to 30 dB above the noise for clean audio. That's why the “busy” threshold is set a few dB above the carpet.

RBW (resolution bandwidth)

The tinySA doesn't measure one exact frequency but a small slice around it: that's the RBW.

  • Wide RBW (e.g. 300 kHz): fast sweep, but the carpet rises and close carriers merge.
  • Narrow RBW (e.g. 10 kHz): lower carpet, fine separation, but a slower sweep.
  • A narrow signal (analogue mic) reads the same whatever the RBW; a wide signal (DTT, Spectera, wideband digital mic) reads lower with a narrow RBW (you only see a slice of it). Always compare measurements made with the same settings.

Analogue, digital, wide blocks

  • Analogue FM mic: a narrow spike (≈ 200 kHz used).
  • Digital mic (ULX-D, Axient D, EW-DX…): a small rounded hump, 200 to 400 kHz wide.
  • DTT / DVB-T2, Spectera / WMAS: a 6 to 8 MHz plateau, flat on top.

Intermodulation (IM)

When several transmitters arrive strongly in a non-linear circuit (receiver front end, antenna amplifier, in-ear combiner, transmitters too close together), they create new frequencies:

  • 3rd order, 2 transmitters: 2A − B and 2B − A (the strongest and most common). E.g. A = 600, B = 602 → products at 598 and 604 MHz.
  • 3rd order, 3 transmitters: A + B − C (weaker).
  • 5th order: 3A − 2B… (weaker still, useful for in-ears on a combiner).

The stronger and the closer together the transmitters are, the stronger the products. The aim is to keep products off the frequencies in use: that's what coordination does (chapter 24).

Selectivity and receiver filter

Every receiver has an input filter that only lets through a slice of spectrum around its frequency (16 MHz, 32 MHz…). Transmitters outside it are attenuated and don't create IM in that receiver. Details in chapter 25.

Antennas, cables, distance

  • The level read depends on the antenna (gain, orientation), the cable (loss: ~3 to 6 dB per 10 m of RG58 at UHF), any amplifier / splitter and the distance. The offset (expert mode) corrects for this.
  • Put the tinySA antenna where the receiver antennas are: you see what they see.
  • A tinySA too close to a strong transmitter overloads (around −10 dBm) and shows fake signals (its own IM): move it away or add attenuation.

10. Step 1 — Prepare at home

1.1 Learn the app in demo mode

  1. Start the app, tick Demo mode (at the top) then ▶ Start. Fake signals appear: mics, TV channels, a jammer that comes and goes.
  2. Do the 🎤 My first show, from A to Z tutorial (🎓 Tutorial). It walks you through each step with the fake signals.
  3. Untick demo mode when you are done (■ Stop first).

1.2 Country and bands

  1. In the settings, Country and plan section: choose your country. It sets the TV channels shown and the « what is this frequency used for » strip under the spectrum.
  2. Bands to sweep: only keep the ranges where your devices are. Classic example: UHF 470–694 MHz for mics and in-ears; add 1.8 GHz (DECT) or 2.4 GHz if you have those systems. Fewer bands = faster sweep = more reactive alarms.
  3. ⚡ Points above the spectrum: leave Auto balanced. The app picks the fineness so a sweep stays fast.

1.3 Your frequency list

If you already have a plan (EazyRF, WWB, WSM, Excel), ② My frequencies › 📥 Import. Otherwise, add by hand (＋ Add) or wait for the site scan to do the coordination (step 4).

For each frequency, the status is the most important setting:

  • « In use » (must transmit): your transmitter must be on it. If it disappears → ABSENT alarm.
  • « Free » (must stay free): a reserve (backup, another day). If someone comes onto it → CONFLICT alarm.

1.4 Device profiles

☰ More › 📐 Plan › 🎛 Device profiles: pick the brand / range of each device. The profile gives the minimum spacing between frequencies, the receiver filter width and the tuning step. Coordination calculations depend on it. If you know the values from your usual coordination software, create a custom profile with them.

1.5 Stages (festival)

In « My frequencies », Sort by stage › group box: ＋ Stage for each stage (Main stage, Club…), ＋ Group for each family (Mics, In-ears, Walkies…). Put your frequencies in them (right-click a stage › assign the selection). The alarm priority is set per group (right-click › 🔔 Priority).

1.6 Save the show

Ctrl+S (or 🎪 Shows › Save): a .tsashow file keeps everything (frequencies, stages, bands, kept scans, schedule). Fill in the festival name, venue and dates (🎪 › Show info): they will appear in the reports.

11. Step 2 — Arriving on site: scan the venue

Why? Before choosing any frequency, you need to know what already occupies the air: TV channels, other RF teams (rental companies, broadcast, security), walkie-talkies, LED screens that « spit ». A frequency that looks free on paper may be taken on site.

  1. Set up the tinySA where your receivers will be (RF desk, side-stage), antenna in the clear.
  2. Choose the port (✔ tinySA) then click ① Scan the venue: sweeping starts and the max hold (orange curve) restarts from zero.
  3. Let it run at least 10 minutes, ideally during light / screen tests (they change the noise floor). The longer, the more the max hold catches intermittent transmitters (walkies, transmitters being tested).
  4. Look at the spectrum (chapter « Reading the spectrum »): 8 MHz plateaus = TV, peaks = transmitters, the bottom = noise floor. Hover to read frequency and level.
  5. Keep this scan: ☰ More › 📍 Scans by location › 📍 Keep (name it after the stage or place). It will be used for coordination and the report.
  6. Optional: 📏 Reference › Capture = the venue's « quiet », for a relative threshold (expert).
Classic trap: scanning at 9 am on an empty site, then finding out in the evening that the regional TV is setting up its links. Scan again once the other teams have arrived, and compare (⚖ in the replay).

12. Step 3 — Several stages: scan each place with the roaming tinySA

Why? On a big site, each stage has its own radio environment: the stage near the road sees the town's TV transmitter, the one in the tent sees the LED screens. By keeping a scan per stage, you can coordinate each stage with its spectrum, or the whole site at once.

3.1 The kit

A tinySA plugged into a Raspberry Pi with a power bank, in a bag, linked over Wi-Fi to the FOH PC. Full setup in the chapter « Two tinySAs, network relay › Portable hunting kit » (install once, relais-raspberry-pi.zip file supplied with each version). With a single tinySA, it is the 1st tinySA (port list › « 🌐 Network tinySA… » › the Pi's address). With two, the 2nd.

3.2 Prepare the web page

  1. On the PC: 📣 Alerts and web page (⑤ or ☰ More) › web page tab: enable it, set a PIN code (4 digits or more) and tick the 🎒 Field view (it is hidden by default).
  2. The page's address is shown (e.g. http://192.168.1.10:8080): open it on your phone, connected to the same Wi-Fi.

3.3 Walking around the site

  1. Arrive at the first stage. On the phone, 🎒 Field view: choose the bag's tinySA, tick the stage you are at then 📡 This tinySA is here. (Several neighbouring stages? tick them all.)
  2. ↺ New scan here: those stages' max hold restarts from zero.
  3. Look at 📈 What the tinySA sees: the live spectrum, the persistence (blue traces that fade: what comes back often makes a thick line) and the stage's max hold in its colour. Wait until the max hold stops moving much (a few minutes).
  4. 💾 Keep this scan, with a label if you like (« front », « stage left », « FOH »). You can keep several per stage, at different spots: they will be merged.
  5. Move to the next stage and start again. Everything is saved on the PC, in the show.

On the PC, clicking a stage in « My frequencies » shows the merge of its scans (dashes); ☰ More › 📍 Scans by location lists them all, and lets you export them (WWB, WSM, CSV) or import a scan from other software (including WWB's native .sdb3 format).

13. Step 4 — Build the frequency plan

The goal: a list where each frequency is far enough from the others, with no « ghost frequency » (intermodulation) on it, and away from what already occupies the venue.

4.1 Choose the profiles

Each frequency has its device (« Device » column of 🧮 Check the plan): spacing and filter depend on it (chapter « Receiver filter and device profiles »).

4.2 Find frequencies: auto coordination

  1. ③ Coordinate the plan › 🪄 Find N compatible frequencies.
  2. Device: the profile of the equipment to tune. How many: the number of frequencies. From … to: the range of your transmitters (written on them, e.g. 470–608 MHz for an « A » range).
  3. Avoid what is busy + the « Spectrum: … » list:
    • stage X: per-stage coordination (its kept scans + its current max hold); offered by default if that stage is selected in « My frequencies », and the new frequencies are filed in it;
    • whole site: global coordination (merge of all scans): for equipment that moves between stages, or a compact site;
    • current max hold: what the tinySAs see now.
  4. 🔎 Search, then ＋ Add to my list. They arrive with the « In use » status.

If the app does not find enough (« the range is full »), it is often physics: too many transmitters on too few MHz. Widen the range, choose a more robust profile (tighter spacing and tolerances), or spread over another band.

4.3 Check

③ › 🧮 Check the plan checks the whole list. Each line marked ✗ says why (« too close to HF 3 », « IM3 of HF 1 and HF 4 »); Replace suggests a frequency that creates no new problem. Check again until there are 0 problems.

4.4 Understand intermod: ④

④ Intermod lists the products (2A − B, A + B − C…) landing on your frequencies, from the most serious (measured above the threshold) to the least (calculation only). The rules at the top choose what is counted; « Like EazyRF » is a good starting point.

4.5 Reserves

Keep 2 or 3 compatible frequencies as « Free » per stage: they will be your backups, and the app warns you if someone moves onto them.

4.6 Set the transmitters and save

Export the list (☰ More › 📋 List › 📤 Export) to set the receivers (or their software), then Ctrl+S.

14. Step 5 — Before doors open

  1. Thresholds (threshold bar above the spectrum): click Auto next to the « busy » threshold: it sits just above the noise floor. « Weak level from »: 10 dB below busy. « TX absent after »: 5 to 10 s.
  2. ⑤ › ▶ Start monitoring; accept to record the session.
  3. 🎤 Line check (Ctrl+L): switch the transmitters on one by one; each frequency seen gets ticked. An « unknown » frequency near a missing one = the wrongly set transmitter.
  4. All « In use » lines must be OK (green). The « Free » ones blue.
  5. 📣 Phone alerts: test sending (📖 Tutorial button for ntfy or Telegram).
  6. Web page for the team: 🎛 FOH view on a tablet, 📋 List on phones.
  7. 🧱 Wall on the FOH's 2nd screen (⛶ full screen).
  8. 🎬 Schedule: if sets change, enter who plays when: mics switched off between two gigs will not trigger an alarm.

15. Step 6 — During the show: monitor and react

The Signal column and the wall are enough to follow along: green = all is well. When an alarm goes off (sound + red line in the alarms + phone):

A mic gets jammed in the middle of a gig

  1. 🆘 Backup frequency: the app suggests frequencies that are free NOW and compatible (choose the stage's spectrum).
  2. Switch the transmitter / receiver pair (or take a « Free » reserve already prepared).
  3. Change the frequency in the list (double-click MHz): the log notes « NEW FREQUENCY ».
  4. After the song: 🔎 hunt to find the culprit.

16. Step 7 — The night and several days

☰ More › 🌙 Festival mode: the app

  • restarts monitoring by itself after a power cut or a crash (if it was running);
  • prevents the PC from going to sleep;
  • can start with Windows;
  • sends a summary every morning (phone + HTML report): the night's alarms, or « ✅ all good ».

Keep the tinySA on mains power (not on its battery). Check disk space if session recording is on (a few tens of MB per hour, shown in the settings).

17. Step 8 — After the festival

  1. ■ Stop: the session is closed (folder with the recorded spectrum, the log and the list).
  2. ⑥ › ⏪ Replay: review any moment (time slider); « 📂 Open a session » for another day; ⚖ compare two sessions.
  3. 📊 Occupancy per hour: the time slots when each frequency was disturbed.
  4. 📄 Show report: a clean HTML document (frequencies, alarms, incidents, scans) to send to the production.
  5. Ctrl+S: the show keeps the per-stage scans and the plan. Next year: 🎪 › Open, and you start again from there.
Part D — Tool reference

18. Preparing a show

Bands to sweep

  • ⚙ Settings › Bands to sweep: tick the bands, or add some (start, stop, step). Presets exist (UHF mics, Spectera, 1G4…).
  • More points means finer detail but a longer cycle. The app estimates how long a cycle takes.
  • ⚡ Points (button in the spectrum bar, in every mode, or ⚙ Settings › Measurement › Points / sweep): ⚡ Auto fast / balanced / detailed (≈ 2,000 / 4,500 / 9,000 points per cycle) or a fixed number per band. Auto balanced is the default. In Auto, the app picks the step from the total width of your checked bands; balanced gives 10 kHz on a small area or the FM band, 50 kHz over the whole 470–694 UHF, 125 kHz over 470–960. Never finer than 10 kHz (a mic carrier is ~200 kHz wide: finer shows nothing more), and never wider than the RBW if you force it (otherwise a narrow signal could slip between two points). The step updates by itself when you add, check or uncheck a band; setting a band's points by hand turns Auto off.
  • Country and plan: pick your country for the right TV channels and allocations.

Your frequencies

  • 📡 Add from the scan (② My frequencies, or ☰ More › 📋 List): the signals the tinySA sees above a threshold (max hold, or live) become frequencies in your list, named Scan 1, Scan 2…, in the scene / group and with the status of your choice. 19. Thresholds and alarms

The three live thresholds (occupied, weak, TX absent) are in the bar right above the spectrum; everything else is in ⚙ Settings › Thresholds and alarms.

Thresholds: when is it busy?

  • “Busy” threshold: above it, the frequency is busy. Set it 6 to 10 dB above the carpet; Auto works it out from the measured noise. Too low = false alarms; too high = a weak interferer goes unnoticed.
  • “Weak” level: the in-between (yellow) zone: mic far away, weak battery, distant carrier.
  • A frequency's own threshold: right-click the row › “Frequency's own threshold” (e.g. next to a DTT transmitter where the carpet is higher).
  • Expert: threshold over reference = busy if it exceeds the venue's quiet spectrum by X dB; window = width around the frequency where the level is read; occupancy = period over which the % of time busy is computed.

Alarms

  • TX missing after: how many seconds without a signal before ⚠ MISSING (never less than 2 cycles).
  • “Too strong” alert (−25 dBm by default, purple line): ⚠ TOO STRONG, overload risk (walkie-talkie right next to an antenna…).
  • Sound alert: a sound when an alarm appears; critical ones replay it until acknowledged.

Alarms that don't ring for nothing

  • ⚙ Settings › Sound › Replay critical ones: every 10 s, 30 s (default), 1 min, 5 min or never.
  • CONFLICT after (3 s by default): a signal on a « must stay free » frequency must last this long before the alarm; a brief peak no longer rings. The log still records it.
  • Short beep when a frequency becomes busy: unticked by default; only real alarms make a sound.
  • 🔕 Mute (alarm panel): mutes the sound for 5, 15, 30 or 60 min, e.g. during a line check or a changeover. Alarms stay displayed and are still sent to the phone; the sound comes back by itself.
  • 📝 What to log: see chapter 31.
  • Expert: Detect unknown signals (peaks not in the list, logged as UNKNOWN).

24. Coordination: plan, intermod, backup

Find N compatible frequencies (auto coordination)

Workflow bar › ③ Coordinate the plan, or ☰ More › 📐 Plan. Like coordination software: pick the device (its filter, spacing, step, chapter 25), how many frequencies you need and the range (your transmitters' band). 🪄 Search finds, from bottom to top, frequencies compatible with your whole list and with each other (spacing, intermod per the rules, filter), on the device's step, avoiding what the tinySA sees as busy (max hold above the « busy » threshold: run ① Scan the venue for a few minutes first). ＋ Add to my list puts them « In use », named « HF 1, HF 2… », in the chosen stage. If the range is full, the app says so (widen the range, or use a more robust profile: tighter spacing and tolerances). Then check with 🧮.

Several devices at once (coordination between devices, e.g. 8 QLX-D + 6 PSM 1000): set the device, how many, the range and the name, then ＋ Add this batch; repeat for the next device. 🪄 Search then finds the frequencies of each batch, all compatible with each other and with your list, each with the rules of its own device. Put the most demanding device first (more spacing, larger tolerances): it gets the pick of the best spots. If the chosen group has several devices (right-click the group › 📻 Devices of this group), one batch per device is prepared automatically.

Check the plan (like EazyRF)

  • 🧮 button or ☰ More › 📐 Frequency plan › 🧮 Check the plan. For each frequency: ✔ compatible or ✗ with the details (spacing too small, 2A − B, A + B − C, 5th order).
  • Default device and Device column: a profile per frequency (chapter 25).
  • Rules: “Like EazyRF” (2 TX ±100 kHz, 3 TX ±50 kHz) by default; the sentence “We check: …” says exactly what is counted.
  • On a ✗ row: 🔍 Suggest a compatible frequency (on the device's step, avoiding what the tinySA sees as busy), then ✔ Replace.

Intermodulation

  • Compute products: what lands on what among your transmitters, with the measured level and a verdict 🔴 🟠 🟡 ⚪.
  • Find the origin of a signal: the source “signals SEEN on the spectrum” (default) = the peaks actually measured (max hold), declared in your list or not. The app looks for combinations of those peaks that land on the interfering signal, most likely first (3rd order, right on it). Let the sweep run for a while first: the max hold catches intermittent transmitters.
  • 👁 Monitor adds products to your list as “Free”.

Stage schedule (who plays when)

⑤ Monitor the show › 🎬, or ☰ More › ② During the show. For each stage, you add slots: group (or the whole stage), day (every day or a specific festival day), start and end (an end before the start = the set ends after midnight). With a margin before (line check, changeover: 15 min by default) and after (encore: 10 min):

  • during its slot, the group must transmit: normal ABSENT alarm if a mic goes quiet;
  • outside its slot, its mics are « 💤 not on stage »: no more alarms for a band that hasn't arrived yet or has already left; if they transmit anyway (soundcheck), they stay « OK »;
  • each stage has its own schedule; a stage without slots is not affected;
  • every change is written to the log (« STAGE CHANGEOVER: Group A → Group B »); the web page's Control room view shows the group in grey « 💤 not on stage ».

The schedule is saved with the show (🎪).

Interference hunt

⑤ Monitor the show, ☰ More › ② During the show, or right-click a row of the log. Put a directional antenna on the tinySA, choose the frequency (taken from the row selected in your list, or from M1) and ▶ Hunt: the app runs a tight live zoom (2 MHz by default, very fast sweep) and plays a beep that speeds up and rises as the level rises, like a metal detector. The level is shown in large type, with the max seen and the gap to the max (« −7.8 dB »): turn around, move, and walk back to the source. « Follow the strongest in the area » if the signal drifts a bit. If it saturates close by, add attenuation.

Transmit mode (generator)

☰ More › ② During the show › 📡. The tinySA Ultra becomes a small generator to test a receiver (its RF meter, its squelch, reception of a frequency), a cable or a splitter: pure carrier, FM (like an analogue mic: adjustable deviation and test tone) or AM, from −115 to about −20 dBm. « Step through my list » moves from one frequency of your list to the next every N seconds: handy to check every receiver in turn. ⚠ Cable only, with an attenuator: transmitting over the air without authorisation is illegal. Monitoring must be stopped (the tinySA can't sweep and transmit at the same time); « ■ Stop transmitting » puts it back into analyser mode.

Backup frequency

Free channels according to the measured spectrum, with no intermod against your list, one click away when a mic gets interference. Only transmitters count for intermod (not « must stay free » reserves), with the receiver filter. With many transmitters, 3 TX products quickly cover the whole band: pick « 2 TX only ». If there is no perfect frequency, the app still offers the least bad ones, in orange, with what's wrong (« intermod ×2: 2×… », « level … ») in the Note column.

25. Receiver filter and device profiles

What is the filter?

Right after the antenna, every receiver has an input filter (preselection) that only lets through a slice of spectrum around its frequency. A receiver with a 32 MHz filter “sees” ±16 MHz; a 16 MHz filter sees ±8 MHz. Anything further away is strongly attenuated.

Why does it change the intermod calculation? A product (e.g. 2A − B) is made in the receiver's front end from the transmitters it receives strongly. If A is 40 MHz away, the filter has already cut it: no product. So, as in EazyRF: a product only bothers a frequency if ALL its transmitters are inside its receiver's filter.

  • The filter figure only says which slice of the spectrum gets into the receiver, hence how many transmitters count in the calculation (16 MHz makes fewer count than 32 MHz). It does not rank receivers.
  • The spacing (minimum distance between two carriers) and the tolerances (margin around an intermodulation product, per type: 2 TX, 3 TX, 5th order; 0 = type ignored) complete the profile.
  • None (0) = all of them count: the most careful choice when you don't know the device.
  • Products made in the transmitters themselves (in-ears on a combiner, antenna amplifier) are not filtered by the receiver: that's why the in-ear profiles also count the 5th order.

Portable hunting kit: tinySA + Raspberry Pi + phone

The PC stays at the RF desk; you walk around with a tinySA plugged by USB into a Raspberry Pi (USB power bank), which shares it over Wi-Fi. Your phone shows the hunt and beeps. Only one tinySA: it goes in the bag, it is the 1st tinySA. Two tinySAs: one stays on the PC and monitors, the other goes in the bag (2nd tinySA).

  1. Get the kit: on the release page, next to the exe, the file relais-raspberry-pi.zip (relais folder: the Pi program tinysa_relais.py, its installer install_pi.sh and the step-by-step guide LISEZMOI.md, in French).
  2. Prepare the Pi (once): Raspberry Pi Imager › Raspberry Pi OS Lite; in its settings: hostname tinysa-sac, the RF desk Wi-Fi, user / password, SSH enabled.
  3. Install the relay (once), from the Windows Terminal, in the unzipped folder: scp -r relais tinysa-sac.local: then ssh tinysa-sac.local, then on the Pi cd relais and sh install_pi.sh. The relay then starts by itself at every boot and finds the tinySA by itself.
  4. On the PC: one tinySA → port list at the top › 🌐 Network tinySA (Raspberry Pi, other PC)… › tinysa-sac.local (or the Pi's IP address), remembered for next time. Two tinySAs → ⚙ Settings › 2nd tinySA = tinysa-sac.local, position name « Backpack ». Then ▶ Start.
  5. On the phone: the web page (📣 Alerts and web page), 🔎 Hunt view. Type the frequency, pick the bag's tinySA, ▶ Hunt (web page PIN). The phone shows the level in large type and beeps (the PC stays quiet). With two tinySAs, the PC's one keeps monitoring all the bands.

Advance coordination with the roaming tinySA (« Field » web view)

Before the festival, walk around the site with the kit and keep each stage's spectrum, from your phone. The view is hidden by default: tick « 🎒 Field » in 📣 Alerts and web page › offered views. Every command needs the web page PIN.

  1. 📍 Where is this tinySA? Tick the stage you are at (one, several, or ☑ All), then 📡 This tinySA is here. Its sweeps then feed those stages' spectrum.
  2. ↺ New scan here: the max hold restarts from zero (when you arrive somewhere new).
  3. Wait a few sweeps, then 💾 Keep this scan, with a label if you like (« stage left », « FOH », « dressing rooms »…). You can keep as many as you like per stage, on different stages.
  4. The « 📊 Kept scans » list shows them all; 🗑 deletes one.
  5. 📈 What the tinySA sees (in the Field view): the live spectrum with persistence (every sweep leaves a blue trace that fades over about fifteen sweeps: a signal that is always there draws a thick bright line, an intermittent one — walkie-talkie, hopping transmitter — a faint trace) and the max hold of each stage in its colour (« Max hold: … » list: all stages, one, or none). A stage's max hold = what the tinySA saw for it since the last ↺ New scan here: that is what 💾 keeps. The Persistence box also exists in the 📈 Spectrum view.

On the PC, clicking a stage in « My frequencies » shows the merge of its kept scans; 📍 Scans by location lists them all (WWB / WSM / CSV export).

Then, coordination per stage or global: in 🪄 Find N frequencies and 🆘 Backup frequency, the « Spectrum: … » list chooses what to avoid: one stage (all its kept scans + its current max hold), the whole site (merge of all scans, for frequencies that work everywhere) or the current max hold. If a stage is selected in « My frequencies », it is offered by default.

The Pi and the phone must stay within the site Wi-Fi coverage. Without Wi-Fi: the tinySA on its own, on its own screen (frequency in the centre, small span, marker on the peak), still works.

Network link status

For a network tinySA (Raspberry Pi, other PC), its throughput and response delay are shown next to its version, updated every 2 s (e.g. « 🌐 18 KB/s · 12 ms »). A tinySA uses a few tens of KB/s: the network is never saturated; a delay that climbs (hundreds of ms) points to a weak or crowded Wi-Fi.

33. Frequently asked questions

The PC does not see the tinySA.

In order: is the tinySA on? does the cable carry data (try another)? is tinySA-App closed? ⟳ next to the port. A tinySA in « USB disk » mode (SD menu) is not a serial port: leave that mode.

The spectrum is empty / flat.

Antenna screwed on? Right band ticked (📶 Bands)? Demo mode unticked if you have a real tinySA? Hover: a level around −100 dBm everywhere = the antenna picks up nothing (wrong socket or antenna unplugged).

I get lots of false CONFLICT alarms.

The « busy » threshold is too low (in the noise): click Auto, or raise it a few dB. An LED screen or a power supply near the tinySA also raises the noise: move it away.

My mic is on but stays ABSENT.

Check that the list frequency is exactly the transmitter's (to the kHz), that the band containing it is ticked, and that the transmitter is not too far from the tinySA (level under the « weak » threshold).

I click a stage and the live spectrum is empty.

No tinySA is attached to that stage (a tinySA attached elsewhere only feeds its own stages). An orange banner says so above the spectrum, with who watches what: click « 📡 The 1st tinySA is here » (it moves to this stage) or « 🌐 Whole site ». In « My frequencies », « 📡 name » next to a stage shows the tinySA watching it; ∅ No stage (My tinySA and their stages) puts it back on the whole site.

Sweeping is slow.

Fewer bands, ⚡ Points on « Auto fast », or a 2nd tinySA. Live zoom only sweeps a small area, very fast.

What is the difference between max hold, persistence and a kept scan?

Max hold keeps the highest level seen since the last Reset (in memory). Persistence (web page) shows what comes back often, fading out. A kept scan is a max hold saved in the show, for a stage, for coordination and the report.

Per-stage or global coordination?

Per stage if the stages are far apart and the equipment does not move: more frequencies available. Global for equipment that moves around (presenters, walkies) or a compact site: frequencies that work everywhere.

The calculations say « problem » but EazyRF / WWB say OK.

Intermod rules and profiles differ from one program to another. Choose your device's profile and the « Like EazyRF » rules; the receiver filter changes the result a lot (Filter chapter).

The Raspberry Pi kit does not see the tinySA.

USB-C data cable, tinySA on, the Pi's USB-A socket (the Pi 4's USB-C socket only powers it). On the Pi: python3 /opt/tinysa-relais/tinysa_relais.py --diag tells you what is missing.

Can I use the app in English?

Yes: ☰ More › 🌐 Langue / Language, then restart. The web page is chosen per person (FR / EN button).

35. Glossary

Every word of RF and of the app, explained simply. The same glossary is in the app (☰ More › Help › 📚 Glossary), with a search.

WordMeaning
HFHere: everything that goes over the air (wireless mics, in-ears, walkie-talkies). In the trade, « RF » means all the wireless links of a show.
Frequency (MHz)The « slot » where a device transmits, in megahertz. Two mics on the same frequency interfere. Example: 606.500 MHz. Show mics mostly sit between 470 and 694 MHz (UHF).
SpectrumThe app's graph: frequencies from left to right, received power from bottom to top. A transmitter makes a « bump » (a peak) at its frequency.
dBmThe unit of received power. The bigger the number (closer to 0), the stronger: −40 dBm is very strong, −90 dBm is weak, −110 dBm is noise. +10 dB = 10 times more power.
dBµVAnother level unit, used by some software (Wisycom…). dBµV = dBm + 107.
Noise floorThe minimum level the tinySA sees even without any transmitter: its own noise plus the venue's (LED screens, lighting…). Anything clearly above the floor is a signal.
SweepThe tinySA measures one frequency after another, from left to right: one full pass is called a sweep. It lasts from under a second to several seconds depending on width and number of points.
PointsThe number of measurements per sweep. More points = more detail but slower. « ⚡ Auto » picks a good compromise by itself.
RBWThe « fineness » of each measurement (width of the tinySA's filter). Narrow = sees small close signals but slow; wide = fast but two close signals merge. Leave it on Auto if you are starting out.
Max holdThe orange curve: the highest level seen at each frequency since the last « Reset max ». It keeps track of a transmitter even if it was only on for a second.
PersistenceEach sweep leaves a trace that slowly fades: what is often there marks strongly, what rarely passes leaves a faint trace (Spectrum / Field web views).
“Busy” thresholdThe red line: above it, a frequency is considered busy (someone is transmitting). « Auto » places it a bit above the noise floor.
« Weak » thresholdBelow the busy threshold: a signal above this level but under « busy » is « weak » (distant transmitter, weak battery, or rising noise).
Must transmit (In use)Status of a frequency where YOUR transmitter must be on. If it goes quiet for too long: ABSENT alarm.
Must stay free (Free)Status of a frequency you keep in reserve (backup, another day's frequency…). If a signal shows up there: CONFLICT alarm.
MISSINGAlarm: a transmitter that must transmit is no longer seen (flat battery, transmitter off, antenna unplugged, artist too far).
CONFLICTAlarm: a signal appeared on a frequency that had to stay free (another team, a jammer, intermodulation).
AcknowledgeTelling an alarm « I've seen it »: the sound stops, the alarm stays displayed as long as the problem lasts.
Intermodulation (IM)When several transmitters are close together, they create « ghost frequencies » by arithmetic (e.g. 2 × A − B). If a ghost lands on one of your mics, it interferes. Coordination is mostly about avoiding that.
IM3 / IM5Intermodulation order: 3rd order (2A − B, A + B − C) is the strongest and most annoying, 5th order (3A − 2B…) is weaker.
CoordinationChoosing the frequencies of all devices so that none interferes with another: enough spacing between them, no intermodulation on them, away from what already occupies the venue.
Frequency planThe final list of frequencies of all your devices, once coordinated.
Spacing (separation)The minimum distance between two carriers so they do not interfere (e.g. 300 to 500 kHz depending on the devices).
Receiver filterThe slice of spectrum that gets into the receiver around its frequency (preselection, e.g. 32 MHz = ±16 MHz). For the calculation, only transmitters inside that slice can create intermod in that receiver. This figure alone does not say whether a receiver is good: its robustness is set with the profile's spacing and tolerances.
Device profileThe coordination settings of a brand / range (spacing, filter, tolerances, step). Pick yours so the calculations match.
Step (increment)The frequencies a device can use go step by step (e.g. 25 kHz): coordination only suggests frequencies your device can be set to.
CarrierA transmitter's signal at its frequency (the peak on the spectrum).
Wide block (WMAS, Spectera)Some recent digital systems use a whole 6 to 8 MHz channel instead of a single frequency: on the spectrum, a plateau instead of a peak.
DTTDigital terrestrial television: 8 MHz plateaus in the UHF band. These channels are taken and must be avoided (TV channel strip under the spectrum).
UHFThe 470–694 MHz band (in France) where most show mics work, shared with DTT.
Stage / groupHow your frequencies are sorted: a stage = a venue or a set, a group = a set of devices (mics, in-ears, walkies…). Optional but handy at a festival.
Kept scanA saved spectrum (the max hold at a given time and place): it is the venue's memory and is used for coordination.
MergeCombining several scans: the strongest level of each is kept, to see the worst case of the whole site.
Roaming tinySAA tinySA carried from stage to stage (in a bag, with a Raspberry Pi) to scan each location, while the PC stays at the FOH.
RelayThe small program that shares a tinySA over the network (on a Raspberry Pi or another PC), so the app sees it remotely.
Line checkBefore the show: each transmitter is switched on one after the other and the app ticks those it sees, to check that everything is there.
SessionThe recording of everything the app saw during monitoring, to review it afterwards (replay).
Show (🎪)A file holding all your work for an event (frequencies, stages, bands, scans…), to reopen the following year.
