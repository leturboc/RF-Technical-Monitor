TinySA Monitor — user manual

Version 7.13 · for the tinySA Ultra on Windows · RF coordination for festivals, concerts and events. PDF version: docs/manual_en.pdf.

In one sentence: the app keeps sweeping your bands with a tinySA, compares what it sees with your frequency list, and warns you as soon as a transmitter goes quiet or a signal shows up where it should not.

1. What is it?

TinySA Monitor does two jobs at once:

  • Spectrum analyser: live spectrum over several bands, max hold, waterfall, live zoom, venue reference curve, TV channel guide and allocations per country.
  • Frequency monitor: 100+ frequencies, each with a status (“must transmit” or “must stay free”). The app works out the state of each one, raises alarms (sound, panel, phone), keeps a log and can record the whole session so you can look at it again after the show.

On top of that: coordination tools (plan check in the style of EazyRF, intermodulation, backup frequency, line check), a web page for the team, alerts on phones.

The band allocations shown (DTT, 4G/5G, aero…) are for guidance only: always check the local regulations (Ofcom, ANFR, BNetzA…) before transmitting.

2. Install and start

Getting the exe

  1. On the Releases page of the GitHub repository, download TinySA-Monitor.exe from the latest version (or from the “Test” pre-release to try new features).
  2. Double-click it. Windows may show “Windows protected your PC” (unsigned exe): More info › Run anyway.
  3. No installation: the exe runs as it is. Your settings are kept in %APPDATA%\TinySAMonitor\.

First start

  1. Plug the tinySA Ultra in over USB and close tinySA-App if it is open (only one program at a time on the port).
  2. At the top, pick the Port: the one marked ✔ tinySA is detected automatically. No tinySA at hand? Tick Demo mode: simulated signals so you can try everything.
  3. Click ▶ Start. The app offers to record the session (chapter 18); then the spectrum fills up and the tinySA version is shown next to the port.
Three display levels (☰ More › 👁 Display): 🟢 Ultra simple (on the very first start) = the spectrum, your list and the alarms, nothing else; 🔵 Simple = + essential settings, the log and sorting by scene / group; 🎛 Expert = every setting (RBW, LNA, attenuator, segments, measurement window, calibration…).
🎓 Tutorial (button on the workflow bar, or ☰ More › 🎓): a 2-minute guided tour showing each part of the screen in order, with a bubble next to it.

4. RF basics

You don't need to be an engineer, but these few ideas avoid 90 % of the bad surprises.

dBm: the received power

  • dBm measures a power relative to 1 milliwatt, on a logarithmic scale: 0 dBm = 1 mW, −30 dBm = 1 µW, −90 dBm = 1 picowatt.
  • +3 dB = twice the power, +10 dB = ten times, +20 dB = a hundred times. A signal at −50 dBm is 1,000 times stronger than one at −80 dBm.
  • Orders of magnitude with a decent antenna: wireless mic a few metres away −30 to −50 dBm, at 30 m −60 to −75 dBm; tinySA noise floor −100 to −110 dBm; DTT from a distant transmitter −60 to −80 dBm.
  • Other units in the app (Settings › Thresholds › Unit): dBµV = dBm + 107 (50 Ω), % like Sennheiser WSM (0 % = −120 dBm, 100 % = 0 dBm). Files always stay in dBm.

The noise floor (the “carpet”)

Even with no transmitter, the tinySA sees a minimum level: its own noise plus the venue's (LED walls, power supplies, Wi-Fi…). A signal is only usable if it clearly stands out of that carpet: that's the signal-to-noise ratio. A mic receiver typically needs 20 to 30 dB above the noise for clean audio. That's why the “busy” threshold is set a few dB above the carpet.

RBW (resolution bandwidth)

The tinySA doesn't measure one exact frequency but a small slice around it: that's the RBW.

  • Wide RBW (e.g. 300 kHz): fast sweep, but the carpet rises and close carriers merge.
  • Narrow RBW (e.g. 10 kHz): lower carpet, fine separation, but a slower sweep.
  • A narrow signal (analogue mic) reads the same whatever the RBW; a wide signal (DTT, Spectera, wideband digital mic) reads lower with a narrow RBW (you only see a slice of it). Always compare measurements made with the same settings.

Analogue, digital, wide blocks

  • Analogue FM mic: a narrow spike (≈ 200 kHz used).
  • Digital mic (ULX-D, Axient D, EW-DX…): a small rounded hump, 200 to 400 kHz wide.
  • DTT / DVB-T2, Spectera / WMAS: a 6 to 8 MHz plateau, flat on top.

Intermodulation (IM)

When several transmitters arrive strongly in a non-linear circuit (receiver front end, antenna amplifier, in-ear combiner, transmitters too close together), they create new frequencies:

  • 3rd order, 2 transmitters: 2A − B and 2B − A (the strongest and most common). E.g. A = 600, B = 602 → products at 598 and 604 MHz.
  • 3rd order, 3 transmitters: A + B − C (weaker).
  • 5th order: 3A − 2B… (weaker still, useful for in-ears on a combiner).

The stronger and the closer together the transmitters are, the stronger the products. The aim is to keep products off the frequencies in use: that's what coordination does (chapter 12).

Selectivity and receiver filter

Every receiver has an input filter that only lets through a slice of spectrum around its frequency (16 MHz, 32 MHz…). Transmitters outside it are attenuated and don't create IM in that receiver. Details in chapter 13.

Antennas, cables, distance

  • The level read depends on the antenna (gain, orientation), the cable (loss: ~3 to 6 dB per 10 m of RG58 at UHF), any amplifier / splitter and the distance. The offset (expert mode) corrects for this.
  • Put the tinySA antenna where the receiver antennas are: you see what they see.
  • A tinySA too close to a strong transmitter overloads (around −10 dBm) and shows fake signals (its own IM): move it away or add attenuation.

6. Preparing a show

Bands to sweep

  • Settings › Bands to sweep: tick the bands, or add some (start, stop, step). Presets exist (UHF mics, Spectera, 1G4…).
  • More points means finer detail but a longer cycle. The app estimates how long a cycle takes.
  • ⚡ Points (button in the spectrum bar, in every mode, or Settings › Measurement › Points / sweep): ⚡ Auto fast / balanced / detailed (≈ 2,000 / 4,500 / 9,000 points per cycle) or a fixed number per band. Auto balanced is the default. In Auto, the app picks the step from the total width of your checked bands; balanced gives 10 kHz on a small area or the FM band, 50 kHz over the whole 470–694 UHF, 125 kHz over 470–960. Never finer than 10 kHz (a mic carrier is ~200 kHz wide: finer shows nothing more), and never wider than the RBW if you force it (otherwise a narrow signal could slip between two points). The step updates by itself when you add, check or uncheck a band; setting a band's points by hand turns Auto off.
  • Country and plan: pick your country for the right TV channels and allocations.

Your frequencies

  • 📡 Add from the scan (② My frequencies, or ☰ More › 📋 List): the signals the tinySA sees above a threshold (max hold, or live) become frequencies in your list, named Scan 1, Scan 2…, in the scene / group and with the status of your choice. Then rename them, sort them, or delete the noise like any other frequency. Wide plateaus (DTT…) and what is already in your list are left out (can be unticked).
  • 📥 Import: plan from EazyRF, WWB, WSM, Excel… The import recognises the columns and suggests names. You can also drag and drop a CSV onto the window.
  • Or by hand: ＋ Add above the list, or right-click on the spectrum › “Add to my list”.
  • Quick naming: ☰ More › 📋 Frequency list › ✏ Name the selection (HF 1, HF 2…), or Ctrl+D to number downwards.

Scenes and groups (optional)

Like folders, to find your way with lots of frequencies: a scene = a venue or a stage (Main stage, Club, TV studio…), a group = an artist or a type of equipment (Vocal mics, Drum in-ears…). Not required: everything works without it (hidden in ultra simple mode).

  • Sort by scene › group box above the list. Clicking a scene or a group = only that is monitored and scanned; Global brings everything back.
  • Right-click a group: alarm priority. Critical ❗ = sound repeated every 10 s until acknowledged; Normal; Info (no sound).

Saving the show (🎪)

🎪 Shows › Save (Ctrl+S) keeps frequencies, scenes, statuses, bands, thresholds, country, position names, custom device profiles, venue reference and details (name, venue, dates, notes). Next year: Open a show, then Save as…. A show does not touch your preferences (colours, windows, alerts, language…).

7. Thresholds and alarms

Thresholds: when is it busy?

  • “Busy” threshold: above it, the frequency is busy. Set it 6 to 10 dB above the carpet; Auto works it out from the measured noise. Too low = false alarms; too high = a weak interferer goes unnoticed.
  • “Weak” level: the in-between (yellow) zone: mic far away, weak battery, distant carrier.
  • A frequency's own threshold: right-click the row › “Frequency's own threshold” (e.g. next to a DTT transmitter where the carpet is higher).
  • Expert: threshold over reference = busy if it exceeds the venue's quiet spectrum by X dB; window = width around the frequency where the level is read; occupancy = period over which the % of time busy is computed.

Alarms

  • TX missing after: how many seconds without a signal before ⚠ MISSING (never less than 2 cycles).
  • “Too strong” alert (−25 dBm by default, purple line): ⚠ TOO STRONG, overload risk (walkie-talkie right next to an antenna…).
  • Sound alert: beep when an alarm appears; critical ones repeat.
  • 📝 What to log: see chapter 19.
  • Expert: Detect unknown signals (peaks not in the list, logged as UNKNOWN).

8. During the show

  1. ▶ Start. Every sweep updates the list, the wall, the markers and the alarms.
  2. Watch the Signal column (or the wall): green “OK” for your transmitters, blue “✔ free” for your spares.
  3. An alarm? The 🔔 turns red and the Alarms panel opens. Acknowledge silences the sound but keeps the alarm (orange) as long as the problem is there.
  4. A strange signal? Right-click it: ⚡ Where could it come from? (intermod from the measured spectrum), 🔍 zoom, ＋ add to my list.
  • Live zoom: double-click a frequency's level or signal, or right-click › 🔍 Live zoom.
  • 📍 Markers: which ones to show (by state, scene, status, alarms always visible, style). Ctrl+M hides / shows them all.
  • 📏 Reference › Capture: the venue's noise floor, to be done before the audience arrives.

🌙 Festival mode (all night)

☰ More › ② During the show, or ⑤ Monitor the show › 🌙 Festival mode. To let the PC monitor on its own at night or all week:

  • Automatic resume: if the app stops while monitoring (power cut, crash, Windows restart), it restarts monitoring by itself the next time it starts, without asking (session recording follows your last choice). Closed on purpose: it restarts nothing.
  • Start with Windows: the app starts when the session opens. For a real recovery after a power cut, the PC must also power back on by itself (BIOS setting « Restore on AC power loss ») and log in without a password.
  • No sleep while monitoring (the screen may still turn off).
  • Daily summary at the chosen time: an HTML report of the events since the previous summary (logs folder › rapports) and a message on the phone (ntfy / Telegram, set in 📣 Alerts): « ✔ All good » or « ⚠ 2 active alarm(s) », with the alarms of the last 24 h, how long it has been running and the tinySAs online. If the PC was asleep at that time, the summary is done as soon as it wakes up. « 📨 Run the summary now » to test.

12. Coordination: plan, intermod, backup

🪄 Find N compatible frequencies (auto coordination)

Workflow bar › ③ Coordinate the plan, or ☰ More › 📐 Plan. Like coordination software: pick the device (its filter, spacing, step, chapter 13), how many frequencies you need and the range (your transmitters' band). 🪄 Search finds, from bottom to top, frequencies compatible with your whole list and with each other (spacing, intermod per the rules, filter), on the device's step, avoiding what the tinySA sees as busy (max hold above the « busy » threshold: run ① Scan the venue for a few minutes first). ＋ Add to my list puts them « In use », named « HF 1, HF 2… », in the chosen stage. If the range is full, the app says so (widen the range, or use a more selective device). Then check with 🧮.

🧮 Check the plan (like EazyRF)

  • 🧮 button or ☰ More › 📐 Frequency plan › 🧮 Check the plan. For each frequency: ✔ compatible or ✗ with the details (spacing too small, 2A − B, A + B − C, 5th order).
  • Default device and Device column: a profile per frequency (chapter 13).
  • Rules: “Like EazyRF” (2 TX ±100 kHz, 3 TX ±50 kHz) by default; the sentence “We check: …” says exactly what is counted.
  • On a ✗ row: 🔍 Suggest a compatible frequency (on the device's step, avoiding what the tinySA sees as busy), then ✔ Replace.

⚡ Intermodulation

  • Compute products: what lands on what among your transmitters, with the measured level and a verdict 🔴 🟠 🟡 ⚪.
  • Find the origin of a signal: the source “signals SEEN on the spectrum” (default) = the peaks actually measured (max hold), declared in your list or not. The app looks for combinations of those peaks that land on the interfering signal, most likely first (3rd order, right on it). Let the sweep run for a while first: the max hold catches intermittent transmitters.
  • 👁 Monitor adds products to your list as “Free”.

🎬 Stage schedule (who plays when)

⑤ Monitor the show › 🎬, or ☰ More › ② During the show. For each stage, you add slots: group (or the whole stage), day (every day or a specific festival day), start and end (an end before the start = the set ends after midnight). With a margin before (line check, changeover: 15 min by default) and after (encore: 10 min):

  • during its slot, the group must transmit: normal ABSENT alarm if a mic goes quiet;
  • outside its slot, its mics are « 💤 not on stage »: no more alarms for a band that hasn't arrived yet or has already left; if they transmit anyway (soundcheck), they stay « OK »;
  • each stage has its own schedule; a stage without slots is not affected;
  • every change is written to the log (« STAGE CHANGEOVER: Group A → Group B »); the web page's Control room view shows the group in grey « 💤 not on stage ».

The schedule is saved with the show (🎪).

🔎 Interference hunt

⑤ Monitor the show, ☰ More › ② During the show, or right-click a row of the log. Put a directional antenna on the tinySA, choose the frequency (taken from the row selected in your list, or from M1) and ▶ Hunt: the app runs a tight live zoom (2 MHz by default, very fast sweep) and plays a beep that speeds up and rises as the level rises, like a metal detector. The level is shown in large type, with the max seen and the gap to the max (« −7.8 dB »): turn around, move, and walk back to the source. « Follow the strongest in the area » if the signal drifts a bit. If it saturates close by, add attenuation.

📡 Transmit mode (generator)

☰ More › ② During the show › 📡. The tinySA Ultra becomes a small generator to test a receiver (its RF meter, its squelch, reception of a frequency), a cable or a splitter: pure carrier, FM (like an analogue mic: adjustable deviation and test tone) or AM, from −115 to about −20 dBm. « Step through my list » moves from one frequency of your list to the next every N seconds: handy to check every receiver in turn. ⚠ Cable only, with an attenuator: transmitting over the air without authorisation is illegal. Monitoring must be stopped (the tinySA can't sweep and transmit at the same time); « ■ Stop transmitting » puts it back into analyser mode.

🆘 Backup frequency

Free channels according to the measured spectrum, with no intermod against your list, one click away when a mic gets interference. Only transmitters count for intermod (not « must stay free » reserves), with the receiver filter. With many transmitters, 3 TX products quickly cover the whole band: pick « 2 TX only ». If there is no perfect frequency, the app still offers the least bad ones, in orange, with what's wrong (« intermod ×2: 2×… », « level … ») in the Note column.

13. Receiver filter and device profiles

What is the filter?

Right after the antenna, every receiver has an input filter (preselection) that only lets through a slice of spectrum around its frequency. A receiver with a 32 MHz filter “sees” ±16 MHz; a 16 MHz filter sees ±8 MHz. Anything further away is strongly attenuated.

Why does it change the intermod calculation? A product (e.g. 2A − B) is made in the receiver's front end from the transmitters it receives strongly. If A is 40 MHz away, the filter has already cut it: no product. So, as in EazyRF: a product only bothers a frequency if ALL its transmitters are inside its receiver's filter.

  • Narrow filter (16 MHz) = selective receiver: fewer transmitters count, easier plan.
  • Wide filter (32 MHz) = more transmitters count.
  • None (0) = all of them count: the most careful choice when you don't know the device.
  • Products made in the transmitters themselves (in-ears on a combiner, antenna amplifier) are not filtered by the receiver: that's why the in-ear profiles also count the 5th order.

14. Line check

🎤 button or Ctrl+L: switch your transmitters on one by one; each frequency turns ✓ as soon as its signal arrives. A summary “14 / 16 OK, missing: …” goes into the log. An unknown signal right next to a missing frequency points to a pack set to the wrong channel.

15. Spectera / WMAS (wideband carriers)

  • ☰ More › 📐 Frequency plan › 📶 Add a block: TV channel or centre frequency + width (6 or 8 MHz). Or right-click a frequency › “Wide block”.
  • “Must transmit”: the median level of the block is used; “must stay free”: the peak.
  • Intermod and the plan check take the width of the blocks into account.

16. Two tinySAs, network relay

  1. Settings › 2nd tinySA: tick “Use a 2nd tinySA” and give its port (COM4) or network address.
  2. On another PC: TinySA-Monitor.exe --relais (or ☰ More › 🔗 Connections › 📡 Network relay), “Share”, then type the address shown (e.g. 192.168.1.20:5025) on the main PC.
  3. Bands table, “tinySA” column: Auto, 1 or 2. ✂ Split cuts a large band in two.
  4. Position names (Stage, FOH…): shown in the alarms (“seen by FOH”).
  5. ⚖ Calibrate the 2nd against the 1st: same signal into both (splitter), ~20 s, offset set automatically.

If one tinySA drops out, the other takes over all the bands (⚠ DEVICE). On a network, prefer a cable to Wi-Fi.

18. Sessions, playback, report

💾 Recording the session (offered at ▶ Start)

At every start, the app asks whether to record the whole session: the spectrum of every sweep, the log and your frequency list, into the folder of your choice (external drive, USB stick…), one sub-folder per session (date + show name). If something happens during the show, you can go back to it afterwards, even if the app was closed in between. The estimated disk space is shown (often a few dozen MB per hour).

  • 💾 Record and start, ▶ Start without recording or Cancel.
  • “Don't ask again” keeps your choice; the Ask at every start box (Log tab) brings the question back.
  • The session closes on its own at ▶ Stop.

⏪ Playback

  • ⏪ button: look at the spectrum at any moment: the last 30 minutes in memory, 📂 a recorded session (with its frequency list and log from that time) or 🕘 the last session.
  • Time slider, fast playback, “Go to” a log event, max hold since the start.
  • 💾 Export…: saves what is being played back (e.g. the last 30 minutes in memory) as a session, into the folder of your choice, to archive or send.

⚖ Compare two sessions

In the playback: ⚖ Compare with a session… overlays the max hold of another session (soundcheck, yesterday…) as purple dots, and lists the extra (new here) or missing signals compared with it. Handy to see what came with the audience or another stage.

📊 Occupancy by hour (risky time slots)

Playback › 📊, ⑥ After the show, or ☰ More. A table: one row per frequency, one column per hour, the % of the time it was above the « busy » threshold. For a frequency that must transmit, that's normal; for a « must stay free » reserve, it's a risky time slot (red cell, summary at the bottom). Hover over a cell for details.

19. The log

  • Log tab: everything that happens, newest at the top; Auto log = one CSV per day.
  • 📝 What to log…: tick the kinds of events to keep: alarms (CONFLICT, INTERMOD, MISSING), TOO STRONG, frequency switching on (BUSY), back to normal, unknown signals, tinySA lost / found, operations (line check, new frequency). Alarms still go off: only the log is filtered.
  • Double-click an “UNKNOWN”: adds it to your list.

23. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
Port not found / “access denied”Close tinySA-App (one program at a time), replug the USB, ⟳ button next to the port.
The tinySA stops answering after a whileThe app retries on its own. After 3 failed attempts: unplug / replug the tinySA (it can freeze, also with tinySA-App).
A band does not fill up☰ More › 🔗 Connections › 🩺 Diagnostics, and send the file.
Lots of false alarms“Busy” threshold too low: raise it (or Auto), check the carpet with ❓ Traces; an overloaded tinySA makes up signals: move it away from the transmitters.
Window lost off-screen☰ More › 🪟 Windows › Standard layout.
The app crashedSend %APPDATA%\TinySAMonitor\crash.log. The settings are saved every minute, with a backup copy.

24. Where are my files?

  • Settings: %APPDATA%\TinySAMonitor\ (config.json + config.json.bak, reference.json, crash.log).
  • Shows: Documents\TinySA Monitor\Shows\ (.tsashow files).
  • Logs, reports, diagnostics: Documents\TinySA Monitor\Journaux\ (can be changed).
  • Recorded sessions: the folder chosen at start (default Journaux\enregistrements\), one sub-folder per session: chunk_*.npz slices, session.json, journal.csv.

25. What is not verified

The app is tested automatically (logic, real interface on Windows, fake tinySA), and has run for 2 hours in the field with a real tinySA Ultra. Not yet proven in real conditions:

  • two real tinySAs at the same time, the relay on a real festival network, a full night;
  • the alarm sounds, a real dual-screen setup; ntfy sending worked for Clément, Telegram not tried;
  • spacing / step / tolerance values of the profiles other than Wisycom, and the ULX-D filter (assumed the same as QLX-D); the ADT filter (unknown);
  • the level of a real Spectera carrier on the tinySA;
  • the EazyRF / WWB / WSM list export formats (generic guesses);
  • finding the origin of intermod on a real festival spectrum (tested on simulated signals);
  • the English interface has been checked automatically, not yet by an English-speaking user in the field.
